# Process primitives

A small base set of process kinds. Everything the owner authors
composes from them by attachment. The owner named two:
instructional and actionable. This file is the research that
asks whether those two are already written down, before anyone
invents a third.

It does not close spec OPEN 1. A recommendation is not a ruling.

## What matches the two named kinds

Instructional and actionable are not new.

- **Dietz informa / performa.** Informa reasons, computes, derives,
  reproduces knowledge. Performa creates original new facts — it
  does the business. That is know versus do, at the production
  layer.
- **Gery instruction / performance.** Training builds knowing.
  Performance support gets the work done in place. The owner's
  "a process is a verb" is Gery's performance side. A process that
  walks the owner through knowing is the instructional side.
- **Searle assertives / directives.** Assertives say how the world
  is. Directives get someone to act. Commissives are the promise
  that a directive was accepted; they are the same actionable
  conversation, one step on.
- **Winograd conversation for possibilities / for action.** The
  first opens knowing. The second coordinates doing.
- **OPM transform versus enable.** An actionable process transforms
  the object it runs against. An instructional process enables
  knowing and does not consume, create, or change that object.
  A persona attached to a process is an OPM agent: it enables
  without being the transformation.

These are one axis, named five times. The owner's two names sit
on it.

## What is not the base set

Do not replace the axis with these. They answer other questions.

**APQC PCF** is a list of what work is about (order to cash, hire
to retire). The rails are not sorted by what the data is.

**BPMN task types** are the mechanism of a step (user, script,
message). A tool, not a process kind.

**Malone's Create / Destroy / Modify / Preserve** are the same
three transformations OPM already has, plus preserve. They
specialise *actionable*. They do not sit beside instructional.
"Brief" as a verb is a specialisation of actionable: it runs
against an object and produces a report. It is not a fourth
kind.

**Gagné's nine events** are acts and scenes of an instructional
process. The owner already named that runtime: a running process
plays out in acts and scenes. That is structure, not kind.

**Davenport's matrix** is about how much discretion the work has.
Both instructional and actionable processes can be expert work.

**Dietz forma** (store, copy, transmit data) is the one extra
kind the literature insists on. It is not being added here. It
maps as well onto the tools rail — a tool does a thing, alone it
is inert — as it does onto a third process kind. That choice is
the owner's.

## The base set, as a reading

Two kinds.

**Instructional.** Produces knowing. Does not transform the object.
Personas attached to it narrate in their own voices. Its runtime
is acts and scenes (Gagné, and the theatrical cut the owner
already used).

**Actionable.** Runs against an object and transforms it: create,
consume, or change. The report is the product, not the process.

Everything else is attachment, or a specialisation of one of
these two. A persona on an actionable process is instructional
overlay: the doing still happens, and it is walked in a voice.
That is composition, not a third kind.

`brief` is authored as actionable. Instruction arrives when a
persona is attached. Do not mint `kind: both`.

## OPEN

Spec OPEN 1 stays open. This file is the research.

To close it, the owner says one of:

- The two kinds are exhaustive.
- Forma / datalogical is a third process kind.
- Forma is the tools rail, and the two kinds stand.

Until that ruling, the rails may be built against the two named
kinds. Malone verbs may specialise actionable later. They are
not a third rail.
